# Proposed Solution

## The flaw in every other answer

**Blockchain does not stop honey fraud. It makes a lie immutable.**

A beekeeper with 50 hives declares 3,000 kg. A traceability chain records that
faithfully, forever, and hands it a trust badge. This is the **oracle problem**,
and it is the unaddressed hole in essentially every "blockchain for agri-
traceability" project — including most of what will be built on this problem
statement.

## Our answer: the sensor layer becomes the oracle

We do not use IoT as a second feature beside the chain. **We make it the thing
that bounds what the chain will accept.**

> Hive telemetry → AI yield forecast → the P90 is signed on-chain as a **mint
> ceiling** → a harvest declaration above it **reverts in the contract**.

Mass conservation is then enforced at every custody hop, and blending records
constituent origin percentages — which produces the EU origin declaration as a
by-product rather than a form somebody fills in.

**DIAGRAM: "Yield-bound issuance" — a single left-to-right chain with a reject branch**
- Node 1: `Sentinel hive node` (weight, in-hive + ambient temp/RH, acoustics, entrance traffic)
- Node 2: `Signed telemetry` (per-frame HMAC, replay-checked) → TimescaleDB
- Node 3: `Quantile yield forecaster` → P10 / P50 / **P90**
- Node 4: `YieldOracle` contract — P90 signed on chain as `maxKg` for that apiary + season
- Node 5: `HoneyBatch.mint()` — decision diamond
- Green edge out of Node 5: `declared ≤ P90` → **batch minted**
- Red edge out of Node 5: `declared > P90` → **reverts: OverEnvelope**
- Caption: the cap is cumulative across a season, so the ceiling cannot be minted twice

## How this addresses the problem, point by point

| Fraud route | What stops it |
|---|---|
| Inflating harvest at source | Declared kg capped by that apiary's own sensor-derived forecast |
| Salami-slicing many small over-declarations | The cap is **cumulative** across the season |
| Injecting honey that never entered | Transfers must originate from the holder of record |
| Diluting during processing | `Σ outputs ≤ Σ inputs × (1 − declaredLoss)` |
| **Diluting at packing — where it really happens** | Jars × net weight ≤ honey that exists **AND** a passing independent lab report exists |
| Photocopying the QR | Per-jar serialised seal + HMAC under a scratch-off, first-scan anchored |

## Innovation and uniqueness

- **Yield-bound issuance.** Others record claims; we bound them. This fuses
  blockchain, AI and IoT into **one loop** instead of three parallel demos.
- **Sentinel economics.** Instrument ~10–16% of boxes and extrapolate, which is
  what makes 2.46 lakh boxes arithmetically possible.
- **Standards-native.** GS1 EPCIS 2.0 events, GS1 Digital Link QR, ISO 22095
  Identity Preserved custody — not a private format only we can read.
- **Evidence discipline.** Our pipeline **refuses to ship a model that loses to
  a coin flip**, and we publish the failures. More on that in Feasibility.
- **Integrates with Madhukranti** rather than duplicating it.

# Technical Approach

## The system as three interlocking loops

**DIAGRAM: three loops sharing a common spine — draw as three stacked bands, arrows returning left**

**Loop A — SENSE → BOUND (the oracle loop)**
`hive node → signed MQTT frame → TimescaleDB → colony-health + yield models → signed YieldEnvelope → HoneyBatch.mint() gate`

**Loop B — CUSTODY (the chain of custody)**
`beekeeper → collection centre → FPO → processor → brand/retail → consumer`, with a gate on every arrow

**Loop C — VERIFY (the trust loop, and it feeds back)**
`consumer scan → first-scan anchor → clone/geo-velocity check → fraud risk score → VACCP testing tier → NABL lab attestation → back into the packing gate`

Loop C returning into Loop B is the part most designs miss: **consumer scans and
lab results are inputs to the next batch's gate**, not just a public page.

## Custody workflow — farmer to consumer, with what is enforced

**DIAGRAM: six columns, three rows each (WHO / CAPTURED / ENFORCED). Put a lock icon on every ENFORCED cell and draw gate 4 with a heavier border.**

| | 1 BEEKEEPER | 2 COLLECTION CENTRE | 3 FPO / AGGREGATOR | 4 PROCESSOR | 5 BRAND / RETAIL | 6 CONSUMER |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Does** | 50 hives, 8 sentinel nodes (16%) | Weigh-in, scan-to-receive | Pools lots from many beekeepers | Filters, sets moisture, blends, packs | Cartons, pallets, shelf | Scans the jar |
| **Captured** | Hourly telemetry; harvest declaration | Lot weight, moisture, container photo | Constituent lot IDs and their kg | Input lots, output jars, declared loss % | SSCC pallet + carton IDs | First-scan time + coarse geography |
| **Enforced on chain** | Declared kg ≤ **P90 ceiling** from that apiary's own sensors | Transfer must come **from the holder of record** — no orphan lots | `Σout ≤ Σin × (1−loss)`; origin % **computed, not typed** | Jars × net wt ≤ honey that exists **AND** a **passing lab report** | Seal binding immutable once issued | Seal must exist, be activated, and not already scanned elsewhere |
| **Whose permission** | None — they took a KVIC subsidy | None — a scan, not an audit | None — FPO is the unit KVIC already funds | **Not needed** — we certify their *input*, not their process | None — seal travels on the jar | None |

**The fourth column is the strategic point.** We never ask to enter a
manufacturer's factory or lab. Gate 4 binds the honey *before* it reaches them
and the seal travels with it. They either buy honey carrying a verified record
or honey that does not — and only the first supports a claim they can defend.

# Feasibility and Viability

## Feasibility — the argument is that it already runs

- **A full vertical slice works today:** simulator → signed ingest →
  TimescaleDB → 6 models → on-chain gates → FPO/processor/KVIC consoles →
  consumer verification → an independent ledger verifier → committed metrics.
- **The device layer is an interface, not a dependency.** Simulator and firmware
  emit identical signed frames, so nothing above MQTT changes when hardware
  arrives.
- **Independently checkable.** `scripts/verify_ledger.py` reads **only chain
  state**; the database supplies the list of batch codes and nothing else, so an
  error in our database cannot make a broken ledger look sound. Result: **16
  batches, 25 invariants, 0 broken.**
- **Cost is the real feasibility question, and sentinel sampling answers it.**
  Per cluster of 50–200 beekeepers: capex ≈ (0.1 × hives × ₹3,000) + ₹12,000
  gateway; opex = SaaS per beekeeper + ₹0.30–1 per jar seal.
- **The money already exists** — NBHM's ₹500 crore outlay and the Honey Mission
  line item. We plug into a budget rather than create one.

## The evidence discipline that separates this from a demo

We tested our own models the way an adversary would, and **published what broke**.

- **Queen detection from real audio failed, and we do not ship it.**
  Leave-one-hive-out accuracy **0.278** against a **0.510** baseline — while a
  careless random split of the same audio scores **0.992**. That 0.992 is
  leakage: the model memorises the recording session, not the bees. Our trainer
  now refuses to mark any model deployable when it loses to the baseline.
- **A promising reframing was killed by its own control.** Since a node lives on
  one hive permanently, we reframed the task as per-hive change detection. It
  scored **0.960 AUC**. Then the control: the same colony, same state, a
  different day separated at **0.999** — higher than the queen comparison. It
  was a day detector. Not shipped.
- **This is not our method failing.** The peer-reviewed *Bee Together* study
  (Sensors, 2024) merged 10 hives and reports the identical collapse: 99.2% on a
  standard split, **34–84%** cross-hive. We audited the full Open Source
  Beehives archive and found **exactly one queenless recording** in it. The
  public data cannot support the claim the field keeps making.

**Why this is a strength, not an admission.** Any team can show a 99% accuracy
slide. We can show *why that number is an illusion*, and we have the measurement
that proves it. A judge who probes our weakest model finds we got there first.

## Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Strategy |
|---|---|
| A beekeeper still lies at source | We shrink the fraud surface, not eliminate it: the ceiling comes from that apiary's own telemetry, sentinels extrapolate to uninstrumented hives, and a high risk score triggers a physical audit and a higher test tier |
| The QR is photocopied | Serialised per-jar seal + HMAC under a scratch-off; first scan anchors time and coarse geography; scan-count and geo-velocity anomalies raise a public warning |
| Why would a processor or brand adopt it? | Four wedges: KVIC funds the boxes and can condition the subsidy; EU 2024/1438 gives exporters a **14 June 2026** deadline we generate the dossier for; honest processors want the gate as a defence against their *own* supply being diluted; verified origin earns a consumer premium |
| Testing cannot cover everything | VACCP risk ladder — screen → C4/IRMS ₹1,200 → NMR ₹3,000 → LC-HRMS ₹8,000, tier chosen by the batch's risk score |
| Rural connectivity | LoRa store-and-forward, offline-first PWA with device-generated idempotency keys, SMS/IVR fallback |
| Low digital literacy | Voice-first vernacular UX, advice sentences not class names, and a trained "Madhu Mitra" coordinator per cluster |
| **Model generalisation** | Every simulator number is labelled as such. The yield P90 **must** be refitted on measured district harvests before it gates anyone's income — a ceiling calibrated on a simulator would block honest beekeepers, the one failure that ends adoption |

## Data provenance is enforced in code

`ml/fetch_datasets.py` **refuses to download** any dataset whose registry entry
lacks a licence, DOI and attribution. Each dataset gets a `LICENSE.txt` and
`PROVENANCE.json` written beside it. We rejected the best hive-weight dataset we
found — 78 German colonies over three years — because it **declares no licence**,
and rejected a GPL-3.0 dataset because copyleft on a trained model is legally
unclear. Non-commercial data is flagged and excluded from anything shippable.

## Social, economic, environmental

- **Social** — restores trust in a product bought largely for health reasons;
  gives smallholders a verifiable identity in the value chain; the Madhu Mitra
  role creates local skilled employment.
- **Economic** — moves margin from intermediaries to producers; protects a
  **USD 177 M** export line a single EU rejection could damage; the domestic

- **Environmental** — early swarm, queenless and varroa detection reduces colony
  loss, and colony survival is a **pollination** outcome long before it is a

Every public bee dataset is *Apis mellifera* in Europe or North America. ***Apis
cerana indica***, common in Indian beekeeping, has **no public dataset at all**.
Every cluster we deploy creates one — a national asset, and a moat.

# PART B — reserve material for the finale and Q&A

## The four questions you will be asked

**"Why blockchain at all? A database would do."**
Multiple parties who do not trust each other — beekeeper, FPO, processor, brand,
domestic regulator, foreign importer — plus an EU regulator who from June 2026
needs verifiable origin percentages, and a liability trail nobody can quietly
edit. Then say plainly what it does *not* solve. That candour scores better than
a defensive answer.

**"What stops the beekeeper lying at source?"**
Nothing stops it entirely and we do not claim otherwise. The ceiling comes from
that apiary's own telemetry, sentinel extrapolation covers uninstrumented hives,
and a high risk score triggers a physical audit and a higher test tier.

**"Your QR can be photocopied."**
A batch-level QR could be. Ours is serialised per jar with a short HMAC under a
scratch-off, first-scan anchored to time and coarse geography, with scan-count
and geo-velocity anomalies raising a public warning.

**"Isn't this just Madhukranti again?"**
Madhukranti is a registry of who exists — ~14,859 beekeepers against 20 lakh+
colonies, no telemetry, no jar-level QR. We measure what those hives actually
produced and refuse claims beyond it. We want to write *into* Madhukranti.

## The ablation result — the best "real science" moment

| Sensor set | macro F1 | pre-swarm recall |
|---|---|---|
| everything | **0.748** | 0.990 |
| no load cell | 0.739 | 0.988 |
| microphone only | 0.693 | 0.990 |
| **no microphone** | **0.483** | **0.365** |
| scale only | 0.433 | 0.426 |

The microphone is the highest-value single sensor and swarm prediction is almost
entirely acoustic. **Permutation importance told us the opposite and was wrong** —
it ranked mass features on top because the 33 acoustic features are correlated
and permuting one at a time barely moves the score. Ablation by sensor *group* is
the honest measurement.

## The conformal calibration bug

Our first calibration returned a factor of exactly **1.000** and did nothing. The
calibration hives shared a weather seed with the fit hives while the test run did
not — and **conformal guarantees require exchangeability**, which a weather shift
breaks. Coverage went 74.6% → 91.5% → **100%** at ×1.0332.

## The join bug that produced a publishable lie

Testing the yield claim against 53 real colonies, the first run reported honey
**R² = +0.136, "beats baseline"** — modest, believable, publishable. It joined on
`beehub_name`, which is the *apiary*: two values for 53 colonies. Every colony
got one of two feature vectors, so the model learned an apiary mean. Correct key
is `tag_number − 200000`; R² became **−0.296**. What caught it was two log lines
that could not both be true. **The dangerous bug is not the one that crashes — it
is the one that returns a plausible number.**

## What we would need to finish the queen detector

**UrBAN** (FRDR, CC BY 4.0): 10 hives, a 15-minute recording every 30 minutes
*continuously* for two years, with queenright/queenless labels and alcohol-wash
varroa rates. Continuity is the decisive property — it gives the same colony on
**adjacent days** spanning a queen loss, the only structure that separates a
queen effect from a day effect. Blocked on a Globus transfer, not a licence.

## Demo script — 9 steps, exits non-zero on failure

`scripts/demo_flow.py`: publish envelope → honest mint succeeds → **inflated mint
reverts** → **cumulative cap reverts** → custody transfer → blending under mass
conservation **and its rejection** → origin percentages → Merkle-anchored seals →
**genuine jar verifies, cloned jar refused**. Each rejection is asserted to come
from the *intended* contract error.
